# Georgia Eviction Laws 2026: Navigating the Dispossessory Process in Fulton County

Atlanta's booming population and diverse neighborhoods make it one of the top rental markets in the Southeast. However, when a tenant stops paying rent, the financial strain on a landlord can be devastating. 

If you own rental property in Fulton, DeKalb, or Cobb County, you must understand the exact legal steps required to regain possession of your property. In Georgia, the eviction process is formally known as a **Dispossessory Proceeding**. While it is generally straightforward, the courts are heavily backlogged, and a single procedural mistake can result in your case being dismissed.

Here is a high-level guide to understanding Georgia’s eviction process, and why partnering with a top-rated Atlanta property manager is the smartest move you can make to protect your investment.

## Step 1: The Demand for Possession

Unlike many other states that require a strict 3-day or 10-day written notice, Georgia law simply requires the landlord to make a "Demand for Possession" before filing a lawsuit. 

*   **What is it?** The landlord must explicitly demand that the tenant pay the past-due rent or vacate the premises. 
*   **The Catch:** Georgia law does not legally specify a timeframe (like 3 or 5 days) for this demand unless it is specifically outlined in your lease agreement. If your lease states a 5-day grace period followed by a 3-day notice, you must strictly adhere to the terms of your lease. 

**Pro-Tip:** Always make the demand in writing (certified mail or posted on the door) to create a paper trail for the judge.

## Step 2: Filing the Dispossessory Affidavit

If the tenant refuses to pay or leave after the demand, you must formally file a **Dispossessory Warrant** (Affidavit) at the local magistrate court (e.g., the Fulton County Magistrate Court). 

You will swear under oath that the tenant has failed to pay rent and that you have made the legal demand for possession.

## Step 3: Service of Process and Tenant's Answer

Once the affidavit is filed, the local Sheriff's Department or a private process server will serve the summons to the tenant. 

In Georgia, the tenant has exactly **seven (7) days** from the date of service to file an "Answer" with the court. 
*   **If the tenant does not answer:** The landlord can file for a default judgment and immediately request a Writ of Possession.
*   **If the tenant files an Answer:** A court date will be scheduled. In Fulton County, due to heavy backlogs, this can sometimes take several weeks.

## Step 4: The Court Hearing and Writ of Possession

At the hearing, the judge will listen to both sides. If the judge rules in favor of the landlord, the tenant is legally ordered to vacate the property.

**The 7-Day Rule:** Even after you win the case, Georgia law grants the tenant 7 days to appeal the decision or move out peacefully. You cannot touch their belongings during this time. 

If the 7 days pass and the tenant is still there, you must purchase a **Writ of Possession** from the clerk's office and schedule a physical eviction with the county Marshal or Sheriff. *Never attempt a self-help eviction (changing locks or removing doors); it is highly illegal in Georgia.*
