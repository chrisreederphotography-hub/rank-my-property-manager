# Navigating Denver's 2026 Landlord-Tenant Laws: What Property Owners Need to Know

If you own rental property in Denver, staying ahead of local and state regulations isn't just about best practices—it's about protecting your investment and avoiding costly penalties. As we move into 2026, the Colorado legislature and the City of Denver have introduced significant updates to landlord-tenant laws that every property owner and manager must understand.

From new transparency requirements in rental pricing to stricter rules surrounding security deposits and mandatory city licensing, the compliance landscape is shifting. Here is your comprehensive guide to the critical 2026 landlord-tenant law changes in Denver, Colorado.

## 1. Security Deposit Protections (HB25-1249)

Effective January 1, 2026, Colorado has fundamentally updated how security deposits are handled. The new legislation, **HB25-1249**, increases transparency and tenant protections at move-out. 

*   **Trust Requirements:** Security deposits are now explicitly legally defined as tenant money held in trust by the landlord.
*   **Itemized Deductions:** Landlords are required to provide a highly detailed, itemized statement for any deductions within 30 days of the lease ending.
*   **Pre-Move-Out Inspections:** A significant new provision allows tenants to formally request a joint walk-through inspection before the lease terminates to accurately document existing damages and give tenants a chance to rectify issues.
*   **"Normal Wear and Tear" Defined:** The law more strictly defines "normal wear and tear," expressly preventing landlords from deducting for things like wall scuffs, minor paint wear, or carpet buckling.

## 2. Rental Pricing Transparency (HB25-1090)

Gone are the days of advertising a low base rent and tacking on mandatory fees later in the application process. 

Under **HB25-1090**, landlords must now advertise the **total rental price** for a unit. This means that all mandatory recurring fees (such as trash, required technology packages, or mandatory amenity fees) must be baked into the advertised rent price rather than listed as separate, add-on charges. This law aims to prevent "junk fees" and give renters a clear picture of their monthly obligations before applying.

## 3. The Denver Residential Rental Property (RRP) License

While not brand new for 2026, the City of Denver is strictly enforcing its **Residential Rental Property (RRP) License** program. If you are renting out a property for 30 days or more in Denver, this license is mandatory.

*   **Mandatory Inspections:** Before applying for your license, you must pass a comprehensive third-party inspection using the city's official checklist to ensure the property meets basic health and safety standards.
*   **Licensing Terms:** Once obtained, the RRP license is generally valid for four years.
*   **Non-Transferable:** Crucially, if you buy or sell a rental property, the license does *not* transfer. The new owner must obtain a brand-new license and complete a new inspection.
*   **Steep Penalties:** Operating an unlicensed rental property in Denver can result in devastating fines—up to $5,000 per violation.

## 4. Specialized Tenant Protections

Two additional bills passed in late 2025 have lasting impacts on leases in 2026:
*   **HB25-1108 (Early Termination Due to Death):** Provides protections for leases terminated early due to a tenant's death, prohibiting rent acceleration or unfair penalties against the deceased tenant's estate.
*   **HB25-1168 (Protections for Victim-Survivors):** Offers specific housing protections for victim-survivors of unlawful sexual behavior, ensuring they cannot be unfairly penalized for rent payment failures directly tied to their circumstances.
