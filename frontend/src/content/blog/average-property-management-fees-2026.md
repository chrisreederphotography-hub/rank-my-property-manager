# The 2026 Guide to Average Property Management Fees

Hiring a property manager is one of the best investments a landlord can make to reclaim their time. But a common question remains: *how much does it actually cost?* 

In 2026, the property management industry has standardized a few pricing models. Here is exactly what you should expect to pay when hiring a top-rated local manager.

## 1. The Monthly Management Fee
The most common pricing structure is a percentage of the monthly collected rent. 
- **Standard Rate:** Typically between **8% and 12%** of the monthly rent.
- **Flat Fee Model:** Some companies charge a flat monthly fee, usually ranging from **$100 to $150 per unit**, depending on the market and services provided.

## 2. The Tenant Placement Fee (Leasing Fee)
When a property is vacant, managers charge a fee to market the property, screen applicants, and sign a lease.
- **Standard Rate:** Usually **50% to 100% of one month's rent**. 
- *Pro Tip:* Ensure your contract explicitly states that this fee is only paid *after* a qualified tenant moves in and pays the first month's rent.

## 3. Maintenance Markups
When repairs are needed, some managers oversee the contractors. Check your contract to see if they charge a **10% to 15% markup** on maintenance invoices for project management. 

## 4. Lease Renewal Fees
To keep a good tenant in place, managers handle lease renewals and rent increases. This is generally a flat fee ranging from **$150 to $300**.
